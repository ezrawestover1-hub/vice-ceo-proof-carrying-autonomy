# Vice CEO — The Tuesday It Already Handled

## 0:00–0:22 — The hook

**[Open on the live Work queue. Let it sit for two seconds.]**

You do not open Vice CEO to give it work.

You open it to see what it already got done.

Most business work is not difficult. It just never stops. A customer needs
help. A good lead needs a follow-up. A policy changes. A small task sits in an
inbox until it becomes a missed opportunity.

Vice CEO is a behind-the-scenes business operator. It handles repetitive
customer communication and follow-through, then brings a person in only when
judgment is needed.

## 0:22–1:05 — Customer support that moves forward

**[Open the prepared password-reset reply. Show the customer request, policy
match, and prepared reply.]**

Here is a normal Tuesday.

Alex needs help resetting a password. Usually, somebody has to find the
message, look up the approved answer, write a reply, and remember to send it.

Vice CEO recognizes the routine request, checks the approved support policy,
and prepares a clear reply connected to Alex’s original message.

The work is ready before someone has to chase it down.

When the business chooses to connect its mailbox and enable this authority,
Vice CEO can send this kind of approved, routine response through the company
mailbox. Until then, it prepares the work for review. Either way, the owner
can see exactly what is about to happen.

**[Pause briefly on the prepared response and the "Ready when enabled" state.]**

That is the difference between a chatbot that writes words and an operator
that moves work forward.

## 1:05–1:48 — Outreach that does not get forgotten

**[Move to the outreach follow-up. Show the approved campaign context, then
the follow-up receipt.]**

Now, a good lead asked a question last week. The conversation went quiet—not
because the business did not care, but because everyone is busy.

Vice CEO keeps the next approved follow-up ready. It works only from an
approved campaign and a consented contact. It stops if the person replies or
unsubscribes. And it records the work so nobody has to wonder whether a
follow-up happened twice.

No sticky note.

No spreadsheet tab that gets lost.

No good lead quietly disappearing.

With delivery authority enabled, that prepared follow-up can be sent through
the configured business mailbox. Without it, it remains ready for the business
to review and send. The owner chooses how much authority to give the operator.

## 1:48–2:22 — The moment a human should take over

**[Open the refund request marked “Needs you.” Leave enough time to read it.]**

But good autonomy is not doing everything automatically.

A refund is not a password reset. It may need context, empathy, and a business
decision. So Vice CEO does not pretend it knows best. It gathers the relevant
facts, marks the request “Needs you,” and gives the owner a focused decision
instead of another messy thread.

Vice CEO handles the repeatable work. People handle the decisions that need
people.

## 2:22–2:58 — Westover EPR: the first real-world deployment

**[Move to the Registry Change Watch or operations evidence. Show a source,
then a change receipt or briefing.]**

Westover EPR is the first real-world example behind this product.

Its registry watcher works in the background, checking approved public EPR
sources for meaningful changes. When something changes, Vice CEO preserves the
evidence, explains why it matters, and prepares the next owner briefing.

That is not the same as a generic alert. It turns a changing public source
into organized follow-through.

The bigger product is the operator pattern: customer service, approved
outreach, compliance signals, and other recurring business work—all moving
forward without becoming another full-time inbox job.

## 2:58–3:33 — Proof, not blind automation

**[Click a reply or follow-up receipt. Then show the hosted evidence view.]**

Every piece of work leaves a receipt: what started it, what policy applied,
what Vice CEO prepared, and whether delivery was enabled.

That means the business is never asked to blindly trust an AI. It can inspect
the work, understand the decision, and choose the authority level that fits its
operation.

This public demo is intentionally safe: it uses synthetic examples and never
emails a real person. The private business workspace is where a company can
connect approved systems and enable its own mailbox authority.

## 3:33–3:54 — Production proof

Behind the scenes, Vice CEO runs as a background system on Google Cloud. It
keeps durable work state, separates public demonstration from private business
actions, and records the evidence behind each outcome.

It is built to be useful in a real business—not just impressive in a chat
